# Areas Database

# Purpose

Stores long-term responsibility domains that remain stable over time.

Areas provide organizational structure beyond temporary projects.

---

# Responsibilities

Areas are responsible for:
- long-term organization
- domain separation
- operational stability
- contextual grouping

---

# Core Principles

- Areas are long-term
- Areas are not projects
- Areas organize responsibility domains
- Areas provide structural continuity

---

# Examples

- Development
- Learning
- Career
- Research
- Personal Growth

---

# Essential Properties

| Property | Type | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Name | Title | Area name |
| Type | Select | Domain classification |
| Projects | Relation → Projects | Related projects |
| Tasks | Relation → Tasks | Related tasks |
| Notes | Relation → Notes | Related knowledge |
| Resources | Relation → Resources | Related references |
| Active | Checkbox | Active status |

---

# Relationship Philosophy

## Projects
Projects belong to Areas.

Areas outlive projects.

---

## Tasks
Tasks may inherit contextual organization through Areas.

---

## Notes
Knowledge may connect to long-term domains.

---

## Resources
Resources may belong to broader domains of interest.

---

# Organizational Philosophy

Areas exist to:
- reduce clutter
- group related work
- maintain long-term structure

Areas should remain relatively stable.

Avoid:
- excessive subdivision
- temporary categories
- project-like behavior

---

# Dashboard Role

Areas are secondary organizational systems.

They support:
- filtering
- navigation
- contextual grouping

Areas should not dominate the dashboard.

---

# Archive Philosophy

Inactive Areas may later be archived if no longer relevant.

Most Areas are expected to remain persistent.

---

# Design Notes

Areas provide the structural backbone for PARA integration.

Unlike projects:
- Areas are continuous
- Areas represent ongoing responsibilities
- Areas support long-term organization
